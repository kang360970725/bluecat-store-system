# Confirmed Decisions

This is the current business-rule register. New decisions are appended with a date. Superseded rules remain visible and point to their replacement.

## Payroll and profit sharing

- Profit above target is progressive: the first 30,000 is calculated at 30%; the portion above 30,000 is calculated independently at 45%.
- The allocation rule is store manager 20%, store partners 30%, headquarters 50%.
- Dujiangyan uses the second payroll scheme; other stores use the first scheme and its cap.
- Headquarters staff and store managers have attendance capped at 100%; ordinary employees may exceed 100%. Excess store-manager/headquarters attendance is converted separately to time off where applicable.
- Multi-position/multi-store attendance must be evaluated together; manual review retains a confirmation entry for attendance eligibility.
- Manual salary adjustment remains available and auditable.
- Monthly-salary proration uses a maintainable fixed-day denominator.

## Position, level, and compensation

- Stores use centrally maintained system position templates; positions are not duplicated for every store.
- Each ordinary store position can define both monthly and hourly compensation because different employees may be hired under either method.
- A single employee does not use both monthly and hourly compensation for the same position in the same month.
- Store basic positions have fixed compensation and no level.
- The centrally maintained ordinary store positions include 收银、外场、客服、吧员 and 机动岗. Active stores inherit these templates so employee onboarding always selects a formal position rather than creating temporary per-store jobs.
- The store-manager position is a headquarters functional position and requires a P level.
- Store-manager base and performance salary are read entirely from the selected P-level configuration; the position has no hourly compensation.
- Employee records do not maintain independent duplicated salary profiles.
- Transfers use the new position compensation from the effective date; monthly calculation is prorated by actual applicable days.
- Concurrent-position compensation has not been confirmed and must not be invented.

## Organization and cost attribution

- Headquarters is an organization peer to stores and contains headquarters functional staff, including store managers.
- Store managers can still manage the corresponding store, but manager payroll must not be counted again as a separate store wage when it is already included in the store management-fee rule.
- Closed stores do not appear in active payroll calculation or ordinary operational selectors.

## Employees, accounts, and permissions

- Employee accounts use a unique mainland mobile number as their login name. Saving employee details requires a valid mobile number and synchronizes the linked login without changing the account ID. Display nicknames default to the employee name. The standalone admin login remains available.

- New V2 accounts use explicit account role assignments. A role groups functional permissions. The account's store scope limits where those permissions apply. Employment store assignments can supply the account's scope when that mode is selected. Position and grade continue to drive employment and compensation.
- Existing employee accounts keep their position-derived grants until an administrator explicitly saves their account roles. The transition is audited and applies the selected roles across the account's authorized stores. Review differing grants across concurrent appointments before switching. Position changes do not overwrite explicit account roles.
- Role definitions and functional permissions are maintained on the Roles and Permissions page. Employee onboarding and editing manage the linked login account and its assigned roles. Role definitions are shared across stores. Existing account store scope still limits data access.
- For linked employee accounts, a historical all-store scope resolves to current employment stores and managed stores. Explicitly assigned store scopes are preserved. Super administrators retain all-store access.
- Store managers can view the ranking of all non-closed stores in Store Center. The top summary covers only their authorized stores. Super administrators see the full-store summary. Store workspaces and detailed APIs remain subject to authorized store scope.
- Store managers may view, add, transfer, and terminate employees only within authorized stores.
- Creating an employee opens a login account by default using the employee phone number.
- Initial passwords must be communicated through an approved secure channel, require change at first login, and must never be written as plaintext in the repository.
- Employee IDs, position codes, and role codes are generated by system rules.
- Employee import skips duplicate or invalid rows and reports total, success, failure, row numbers, and reasons.
- Operational tasks target a store-position employee pool, not a named employee. Only positions with current active employees are dispatchable. Result-copy recipients are selected from active login accounts that hold task-management permission for every selected store; they receive a notification after result submission and gain no claim or execution permission from being copied.
- The store-manager role holds operational-task management permission within its existing authorized-store scope. This permits managers to manage and receive copied task results for their own stores without granting cross-store access.
- Operational tasks may also target a store-scoped executor group. The initial `门店管理组` is maintained independently from payroll positions and authorization roles; membership determines task visibility and shared execution only. One member's successful submission completes the group responsibility for that store.
- 2026-11-27 trial rule: active V2 login accounts and the retained admin can publish ordinary one-time or recurring tasks to any valid operating-store target. The publisher may pause, resume or archive their own recurring templates. Task review, rectification, reporting and executor-group configuration follow their existing scoped permissions. A recurring template pauses when its sponsoring account or target is invalid.
- Task publishers can choose a suggested category or enter a custom one in the same form. Recurring tasks inherit their template category. Archiving a recurring template stops future dispatches and preserves published tasks.
- Publishers may attach up to three reference images per task or recurring template. Reference images use private MinIO and are separate from employee completion evidence. The publisher, eligible employees and scoped task managers can read them through the authenticated API.
- Employees may opt in to one WeChat task reminder at a time. The application uses the current bound account and rechecks store and target membership before sending. A missed reminder leaves the task available in the mini-program. Ambiguous send results are logged and skipped to avoid duplicate sends.
- This release uses in-app assignment messages for both immediate and recurring tasks. Task and message creation share one transaction. Only eligible active accounts at dispatch receive the message, and inbox reads recheck current task scope. The WeChat template remains unconfigured.
- New task checklists are structured items with a stable ID and either direct confirmation or one required item-level photo. Existing string checklists and task-level evidence remain readable for history. A task or recurring template explicitly chooses whether submission completes the task automatically or requires manager review; auto-completion still records the actual employee, submission time and evidence.
- Explicit employee claiming is no longer required for normal execution. Submission atomically records the first valid employee from the target position or executor group; concurrent later submissions are rejected. The legacy claim endpoint remains temporarily compatible with existing clients.
- New task evidence remains private in the configured MinIO private bucket and is never included in CSV exports. The API validates authorization before serving an object; clients never receive storage credentials or permanent public object URLs. MySQL retains the object key, checksum, size, dimensions and task/audit relationships. Historical evidence already stored as MySQL BLOBs remains readable through a dual-read path and is not bulk-migrated during this switch. Source uploads are limited to 5 MB each and 24 MB per request, re-encoded to bounded JPEGs no larger than 768 KiB each, and capped at 9 MB compressed per submission. Mini-program item photos use employee-scoped object drafts that expire after 24 hours and are cleaned hourly through a retryable cleanup claim, so a failed delete or interrupted worker is retried instead of losing its cleanup record. Production deployment must verify signed private-bucket upload, checksum-verified download and deletion before applying migrations. Submitted and returned evidence remains retained for audit and is not automatically deleted.

## Costs and historical corrections

- Actual profit and configured profit targets are read from existing monthly data and are not freely editable in payroll trial calculation.
- Existing historical cost records are not deleted through ordinary business operations.
- Authorized users may add missing month-end cost items and make auditable historical corrections because start-date changes or delayed statistics can affect past months.
- Recalculation and development testing must remain repeatable without producing duplicate cost facts.

## Data and crawler integrity

- X Manager raw responses are retained append-only or versioned before normalization and calculation.
- Business time, settlement time, update time, and crawl time are distinct; crawl time cannot replace business attribution time.
- Cross-source records are associated by business identity and rules, not removed merely because amounts match.
- Every exclusion/classification rule preserves a rule identifier, reason, source record, and version.
- Store-specific reconciliation gaps must not be hidden with hard-coded balancing values.
- The only permitted data flow is third party → DATAN-platform → versioned `/bridge/bluecat/v1/*` API → Store V2 snapshots/facts → Store business calculations → frontend.
- `externalStoreId` (`snbid/account`) is the only stable cross-system store key. Internal `storeId` belongs exclusively to Store, and `V2DataSourceConnection` is the only target formal mapping model.
- Collector discovery must never create, reopen, classify, or code a formal Store record. New external stores remain unmatched until the Store business process confirms the mapping.
- Read-only Bridge APIs and authenticated/audited/idempotent task-control APIs are separate contracts. Ordinary page reads must not trigger collection, backfill, or correction.
- V2 is the only active business implementation. The legacy Store/Org/Performance/Scoring runtime modules, legacy frontend pages, `/v2/x-manager/*`, direct `/tasks/*`, unversioned Bridge consumers, and the bundled collector patch have been removed. Historical business tables remain auditable and are not physical production-deletion authorization.
- `V2DataSourceConnection` is the only formal external-store binding. The migration copies resolvable legacy bindings by matching the already-associated legacy store code to an existing non-closed V2 store, keeps unresolved entries pending, and renames the legacy mapping table as a read-only archive.
- All Bridge reads require a dedicated service token. Collection control uses a separate V1 task-control token plus operator, request and idempotency identities; ordinary reads never trigger collection.
- Store must not expose generic row-ingest endpoints for collector revenue data. Source rows enter Store only through the authenticated, versioned Bridge synchronization path; source-to-snapshot conversion helpers are internal implementation details.
- External-store candidates are reviewed in Store's V2 operations configuration and may only be bound to an existing non-closed formal store. New formal stores must be created through the store-center workflow before binding. This supersedes the short-lived create-and-bind shortcut so that store creation has one validation and maintenance path. Discovery remains read-only with respect to formal stores, and duplicate store or external-ID bindings are rejected.

## Operations dashboard delivery

- Current-month revenue forecasts in the store workspace and store-center ranking project the daily average through yesterday over the full calendar month. Both the revenue and elapsed-day inputs exclude today in Asia/Shanghai, including today's OTA redemptions. The store center and WeCom report use the ranking's forecast revenue for estimated profit and completion rates. Historical rankings use the actual full-month revenue. The workspace displays no estimate without settled data or a positive target.
- The canonical Store repository is the only implementation base for the operations dashboard. The previous local application may be discarded and must not be merged back as a competing runtime or data model.
- Dashboard work is additive: reuse Store master data, permissions, revenue snapshots, OTA records, costs, and audit conventions before introducing new models or endpoints.
- When required dashboard metrics are absent, the X Manager read-only collection scope and data contracts may be extended. Existing session checks, source traceability, narrow endpoint allowlists, and append-only/versioned raw evidence remain mandatory.
- Changes that alter existing revenue, cost, payroll, historical attribution, exclusion, or reconciliation logic require explicit approval before implementation. New metrics must not silently redefine an existing metric.
- Material dashboard work must update `docs/CURRENT_STATE.md` and the dashboard scope document in the same change so parallel contributors can see ownership, changed surfaces, open assumptions, and verification status.
- Dashboard access uses `performance:view`. Non-super-admin single-store and multi-store responses are restricted to the account's accessible store IDs, and closed stores are excluded.
- Usage analytics use X Manager surf-detail `cEffect` as the effective usage amount and do not relabel it as settled cash revenue. Session duration and effective amount are split proportionally across hour/day boundaries. Store and area capacity use the union of machines in the latest versioned rate-area snapshot and distinct machines actually observed during the selected period; this prevents a stale snapshot from omitting newly introduced machines. Until maintenance/disabled interval facts are collected, every unioned machine is assumed available 24 hours per natural day and the UI must disclose that limitation, snapshot date, and any observed machine missing from the snapshot.
- Traffic operations are privacy-safe and read-only. DATAN hashes the available encrypted-card identifier inside MySQL, falls back to the source card identifier only when needed, and returns only aggregate counts; neither the raw identifier nor its hash may cross the Bridge. Arrival is a session starting in the selected range, departure is a session ending in it, average stay is based on sessions starting in it, and repeat visitors have visits on at least two distinct dates in the range. `采集期首次` means no earlier collected visit, frequent means at least four visit dates in the selected range, and dormant return means the previous collected visit was at least 30 days before the range start.
- Seat-rate ranking is calculated only across non-preparing, non-closed stores in the caller's `performance:view` scope. Strategy cards are deterministic recommendations derived from the selected period and scoped-store medians; they never execute discounts, send messages, or mutate business data. The default period ends yesterday to avoid treating an incomplete current day as an operational trough.

## Generated weekly and monthly reports (2026-10-19)

- Weekly and monthly reports use independent presentation templates while sharing isolated calculation primitives. Only completed natural periods are shown by default; Monday-Sunday is one week and a calendar month is one month.
- Reports are generated snapshots, not editable live views. Ordinary users may view and export; only super administrators may generate or regenerate. Regeneration must explicitly choose whether to resynchronize the source period.
- Automatic generation runs at 10:00 Asia/Shanghai every Monday and on the first day of every month. Technical failures retry after 30 seconds for a total of three attempts, then remain failed for manual handling.
- Incomplete source coverage does not block successful report generation and is not exposed as missing-date annotations in the report image. Automatic first generation nevertheless performs one missing-date synchronization attempt; manual regeneration never synchronizes unless selected by the operator.
- Machine efficiency uses actual operating days and machine-days. Stores operate 24 hours and all registered machines are considered available. Mid-period openings and closures use their effective dates without comparison normalization.
- New member means newly added BlueCat members. Online-new share is online new members divided by new BlueCat members. Visiting members are distinct members, not visits. Remaining privilege-member attribution details are isolated for later operator refinement.
- Net-fee income is X net-fee income plus group-purchase face value. Product income includes mapped drinks, food, and equipment rental and excludes cigarettes and additional products. Main business income is net-fee income plus that product income.
- Group purchases are reported only as `网费团购` and `包时团购`; package-time keywords take priority, `新会员｜50网费` belongs to net-fee group purchase, and cross-platform amounts are aggregated while channel totals remain available.
- The initial product mapping is shared by all stores and uses the latest rule when a report is regenerated. X categories containing 水吧/饮品/咖啡/奶茶/果汁 map to drinks; 副食/方便面/本吧套餐/餐食 map to food; 香烟 maps to cigarettes; remaining product sales map to additional products.
- The report page exports one full single-store PNG. PDF, Excel, WeCom delivery, multi-store report comparison, and package-sales ranking are outside this delivery.
- Machine count is sourced from X Manager `基础 → 费率设置 → 门店费率设置 → 区域设置`: collect every area's assigned computer number and count distinct numbers across the store. This is low-frequency store master data, synchronized only by an explicit super-administrator action (and may later be reused by the binding workflow), never by the daily report scheduler. Empty or failed source responses must retain the existing Store value rather than overwrite it with zero.

## Product-category synchronization (2026-10-27)

- The performance-split category selector mirrors the latest non-empty product display-category set published by DATAN. Display categories come from X Manager's `show_product_type_name`; `product_type` / `product_type_name` are product types and must not appear in this selector. Categories absent from the display-category set become inactive for current selection and calculation, while their rows and historical exclusion snapshots remain available for audit.
- An empty or unavailable category source must fail synchronization and preserve the current active set; it must never be reported as a successful synchronization or clear current configuration.
- When DATAN changes a category's technical key but its normalized display name is unchanged, Store carries forward the most recently saved exclusion setting for that name. Store does not guess mappings for renamed categories.
- An explicit category synchronization first runs the store-scoped `products` collection task so DATAN refreshes the complete display-category filter snapshot, then reads and applies that snapshot. Ordinary category reads remain side-effect free.
- When DATAN reports `login_expired`, Store must preserve that state instead of reducing it to a generic collection error. An authorized update flow opens the task-control-protected Bridge QR login session, polls through Store, and resumes the interrupted category synchronization after confirmation; Bridge never exposes the resulting access token to the browser.
- The exclusion selector is the union of the current display-category set and categories that actually occur in the selected current month's sales. A category absent from the latest selector remains visibly marked as a historical category when it has current-month sales, and its current-month amount remains selectable for exclusion.
- Category synchronization preserves an exclusion setting by stable source category ID before falling back to normalized display name. Renaming a category with the same source ID must not clear its setting; inactive historical categories retain their setting and participate in recalculation when selected.
- Saving the current-month exclusion selection recalculates daily and shift snapshots from source sales using every selected current and historical category ID. Previous settled months remain immutable unless an explicitly authorized historical correction is performed.

## Historical product-category correction (2026-11-05)

- Authorized users may submit product-category exclusion corrections for a selected historical month. A historical submission recalculates that month's daily and shift exclusion snapshots only; it must not overwrite the current/future default category selection.
- Historical corrections continue to use the existing `performance:update` permission until a dedicated correction permission is introduced. The snapshot source metadata records the correction type, operator and correction time so the later permission split does not lose auditability.
- Recalculation is idempotent and uses the source sales facts for the selected month. Future months remain non-editable, and ordinary category synchronization does not silently rewrite historical selections.

## Weekly/monthly report refinement (2026-11-02)

- This supersedes the 2026-10-19 new-member definition: report `新增会员` is the sum of newly added BlueCat members and newly added Goddess/Student privilege-card members. Daily-new-member, new-member share, and online-new-member share use the same combined denominator; the privilege-card metric remains as a visible breakdown.

## Store workforce scheduling (2026-11-19)

- Workforce scheduling belongs to the store workspace. The default store-manager role receives `schedule:view` and `schedule:update` and manages only an authorized store; a super administrator may manage every store. Operations configuration is not the primary scheduling workspace. Custom roles still receive scheduling access only when those permissions are explicitly assigned.
- Cashier and floor-service staffing use three people per position when the store follows the standard rotation. The standard six-day cycle is `day, day, night, night, rest, rest`; the three employees are staggered by zero, two and four days so one person covers day, one covers night and one rests each day.
- Standard day shift is 09:00–21:00. Standard night shift is 21:00–09:00 on the next day. A rest day is explicit schedule data, not an empty cell; an empty cell means the employee has not yet been scheduled.
- Busy stores may operate bartender, flex and customer-service positions. Bartender and flex shifts are normally 10 hours, customer-service shifts are normally 8 hours, and these start/end times are configured by each store according to its own peak period. These durations are planning guidance rather than payroll-formula changes.
- Bartender, flex and customer-service employees normally receive four rest days per calendar month. The first delivery fills a selected fixed shift and lets the manager explicitly mark four rest days in the monthly matrix; it does not guess peak days.
- A draft month can inherit the preceding published or locked schedule. Explicit work succession maps a former employee to one current store employment from an effective date so a replacement can inherit the former employee's schedule without rewriting historical assignments.
- A shift definition already referenced by schedule history is versioned when edited: the historical definition is disabled and retained, while a new active definition is created. Published attendance history must not change because a store later edits its working hours.
- Ordinary and overtime assignments are distinct. A person already working a regular shift that day cannot also be selected for overtime. Rest, work and overtime remain auditable and attendance only exposes published or locked non-rest assignments.
